QUESTIONS NEW CREATORS ASK

You're not the only one.

Podcasting has a learning curve. These are some of the questions almost every beginner asks.

Do I need an expensive microphone to start?

No. A basic USB microphone or even a modern phone can be enough to learn the process. Prioritize a quiet room, clear speech, and a test recording before spending money.

Can I record a podcast with just my phone?

Yes. Use the voice recorder available on your phone, keep it stable, speak at a consistent distance, and record somewhere quiet. A simple wired headset microphone can also help.

How long should my episodes be?

There is no universal ideal length. Make the episode as long as it needs to deliver its promise without padding. A focused 10-minute episode can be more useful than an unfocused hour.

Do I have to publish every week?

No. Choose a schedule you can sustain. Monthly, twice a month, seasonal, or limited-series publishing can all work when expectations are clear.

What if I don't like the sound of my voice?

That feeling is common. Your recorded voice sounds different from the voice you hear internally. Practice with short recordings and focus on being understandable and genuine rather than sounding perfect.

Can I record with someone in another city or state?

Yes. Remote recording tools can capture each participant's audio separately. Ask everyone to use headphones, find a quiet room, and make a short test recording first.

Do I need to publish on every podcast app?

Not manually in every place. A podcast host can provide an RSS feed that supported listening platforms use to receive your show and new episodes. Check each platform's submission and availability requirements.

Can I use my favorite song as intro music?

Not automatically. Music is generally protected by copyright. Get the necessary permission or use music with a license that clearly allows your intended podcast use. Keep a copy of the license terms.

Can I make money from podcasting right away?

Usually not. Most shows need time to develop consistent content and an audience. Treat monetization as a possible later stage, not a promise or requirement for starting.

What if nobody listens to my first episode?

That is a normal possibility for a new show. Your first episodes are also practice. Share them with people who may genuinely care about the subject, learn from feedback, and keep improving.
