Creator independence

Audience Independence & Portability

Your podcast should be able to grow beyond any single hosting provider or listening app. CrowRules is designed to help creators publish through an RSS feed and build a recognizable show and brand that they can promote across the web.

Your show and your creative work

You retain the rights you own in your podcast name, brand, artwork, recordings, and other creative work. Uploading or hosting content on CrowRules does not, by itself, transfer your copyright to CrowRules. Make sure you have permission for any music, images, guest contributions, or other material you do not own.

Distribution across platforms

Your show's RSS feed can be submitted to multiple podcast directories that accept it. You can also promote your show through your own website, social accounts, and newsletter. Each directory has its own listing, review, and account systems, so availability and approval are controlled by that service.

Building relationships with listeners

You can invite listeners to connect through channels you manage, such as your website, social pages, or an opt-in newsletter. An RSS feed is not a mailing list: it does not reveal the personal identities or email addresses of everyone who listens, and followers, reviews, subscriptions, and listening histories held by Spotify, Apple Podcasts, or another service do not automatically transfer to a different platform.

If you decide to leave CrowRules

Your show identity and the rights you own remain yours. Moving a show to another host requires a planned transition. The process may depend on access to your episode audio and feed information, the receiving host's import tools, and whether the current feed can be redirected using a supported permanent redirect. Do not assume that directory followers or platform analytics can be moved.

Before changing hosts, contact CrowRules so the available export and feed-transition options can be reviewed with you. We will not promise a completed migration, feed redirect, or transfer of third-party followers unless that capability is confirmed for your show.

How it works

From your first show to podcast directories

  1. Create your show. Add your title, description, category, artwork, language, and creator details.
  2. Publish an eligible episode. Your show must be published and have at least one published free audio episode with a working public audio file before its feed is ready for directory submission.
  3. Enable your CrowRules RSS feed. Open Hosting & Distribution in Creator Studio and save the generated feed URL to your show.
  4. Submit your feed. Use the same RSS URL when submitting your show to Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube Music, Amazon Music, and other supported directories.
  5. Track your listings. Update each directory's submission status and save its public show URL in your Distribution Center.
A clear distinction

What “free” means

CrowRules does not charge creators for podcast hosting or for generating their RSS feed. Directory services have their own submission, review, eligibility, and account requirements. Those third-party platforms operate independently, and CrowRules cannot guarantee acceptance or placement.

Free hosting and RSS feed access do not mean every third-party service or optional paid feature is free. Any separate paid service will be identified separately before you choose to use it.

Professional publishing checklist

Give every episode a strong launch

  • Make the show easy to recognize. Use a consistent show name, clear description, category, creator/publisher name, and readable square artwork.
  • Make each episode easy to find. Write a specific episode title and useful description. Add the release date and season or episode number when relevant.
  • Make listening accessible. Consider publishing transcripts, adding chapter markers where supported, and explaining important audio-only information in the episode description.
  • Protect audio quality. Listen to the finished file before publishing. Check that speech is clear, levels are comfortable, edits are clean, and the public audio link plays from another device.
  • Publish consistently. Choose a realistic release schedule and tell listeners when to expect new episodes. Consistency is more valuable than promising a schedule you cannot maintain.
  • Promote every release. Share a direct episode link, a short preview, and a clear call to action on the channels you manage.
Rights, safety & trust

Publish responsibly

Creators are responsible for the material they upload and for securing the permissions needed to use it. Before publishing, make sure you have the rights or licenses for music, sound effects, photographs, artwork, clips, and guest contributions. Do not assume that crediting an artist is the same as obtaining permission.

  • Get clear permission from guests before recording and publishing their voices, names, or likenesses.
  • Disclose sponsorships, paid endorsements, affiliate relationships, and material connections clearly when they apply.
  • Respect privacy. Do not publish private contact details, confidential information, or another person's sensitive information without a lawful basis and appropriate permission.
  • Do not upload unlawful, deceptive, infringing, or abusive material. Follow applicable laws and the policies of each directory where you submit your show.
  • Keep copies of your original recordings, finished episode files, artwork, descriptions, and important show details in storage you control.

This is a practical publishing checklist, not legal advice. Review applicable laws, licenses, directory rules, and the CrowRules policies that govern your account.

Know what the numbers mean

Audience growth & analytics

Podcast directories and hosting providers may report different metrics and use different definitions for downloads, plays, listeners, followers, and engagement. Numbers can be delayed, filtered, estimated, or unavailable depending on the platform.

Use analytics to understand broad trends—not as a guaranteed count of unique people or a promise of income. CrowRules cannot control the reporting methods, recommendation systems, ranking decisions, or audience data held by third-party listening services.

For a healthier audience relationship, invite listeners to follow your show and opt in to updates through a channel you manage. Never treat a directory follow as permission to email someone.

Frequently asked questions

Podcast hosting questions

Does CrowRules automatically place my show on Spotify or Apple Podcasts?

No. Your RSS feed gives you a way to submit your show to directories that accept RSS feeds. You generally create or use your own directory account, submit the feed, and complete any review or verification steps required by that service.

Can I use the same RSS feed with more than one directory?

Yes. The purpose of an RSS feed is to provide a consistent source of show and episode information for multiple compatible podcast directories. Each directory may have its own rules and approval process.

Will my listeners' email addresses appear in my RSS feed?

No. An RSS feed is a public publishing format, not a subscriber contact list. It does not give you the personal email addresses or identities of everyone who follows or listens through a directory.

Does a listing guarantee more listeners or revenue?

No. Directory availability does not guarantee discovery, rankings, audience growth, sponsorships, or income. Those outcomes depend on many factors outside CrowRules' control.

What should I do before moving my show to another host?

Keep your original audio and show assets, then contact CrowRules before changing hosting or deleting anything. We can review the options available for your feed and explain what can and cannot be transferred. Do not change the feed address in directory accounts until a migration plan is confirmed.
